Finding Music

Everything in your library can be found from the Music screen. Choose Music from the main menu, and you can look by playlist, artist, album, song title, genre or composer, with audiobooks kept apart.

  • Scroll a List
  • Go Back
  • Find a Song by Its Title
  • Find an Artist’s Songs
  • Find an Album
  • Use “All”
  • Find Songs by Genre or Composer

Your music is sorted by the details you give each song. The more you fill in, the more ways you can find it.

To Scroll a List

Turn the click wheel: clockwise to move down the list, anticlockwise to move up.

When a list is longer than the screen, a scroll bar appears at its right, and the list scrolls as the highlight reaches the top or the bottom.

OR

Press the Down Arrow or Up Arrow key.

To Go Back

Click MENU, at the top of the click wheel.

You go back one screen, to the list you came from, with the same item highlighted.

OR

Press the Delete key.

On the main menu, MENU does nothing: there’s nowhere further back to go.

To Find a Song by Its Title

Choose Songs from the Music screen.

Every song in your library is listed in alphabetical order of title. A leading “The” is ignored, so “The Long Way Round” is listed under L.

Turn the click wheel to the song, and click the centre button to play it.

Audiobooks have a list of their own, and aren’t listed here.

To Find an Artist’s Songs

Choose Artists from the Music screen.

Choose an artist.

You see the artist’s albums.

Choose an album to see its songs, in track order, or choose All to see every song by that artist.

To Find an Album

Choose Albums from the Music screen.

Every album in your library is listed in alphabetical order.

Choose an album.

Its songs are listed in track order, as on the record.

To Use “All”

The Artists, Albums and Genres lists each begin with All.

Choose All at the top of the list.

All gathers up everything the list leads to: All in Genres lists every artist; All in Artists, every album; and All in an artist’s albums, every song by that artist.

The Composers list has no All.

To Find Songs by Genre or Composer

Choose Genres from the Music screen, and then a genre.

You see the artists with songs in that genre; choose one for their albums, and then the songs.

Or choose Composers, and then a composer.

That composer’s songs are listed straight away.

Playlists and Audiobooks

The playlists you’ve made, and your audiobooks, each have a list of their own in the Music screen.

  • Play a Playlist
  • Find an Audiobook
  • Mark a Song as an Audiobook

To make and change playlists, see “Working With Playlists” later in this chapter.

To Play a Playlist

Choose Playlists from the Music screen, and then a playlist.

Its songs are listed in the order you added them.

Choose a song.

It plays, followed by the rest of the playlist, in order.

OR

Highlight the playlist and click the bottom of the click wheel to play it from the start.

To Find an Audiobook

Choose Audiobooks from the Music screen.

Every song marked as an audiobook is listed here, and only here: audiobooks aren’t listed under Songs, Artists or the other lists, and they’re never shuffled.

A title too long for the screen ends in “…”.

To Mark a Song as an Audiobook

Highlight the song in a list, and choose Edit Song from the Edit menu.

Click the Audiobook box, so that a cross appears in it, and click Save.

The song moves from the other lists to Audiobooks.

You can mark a song as an audiobook when you import it, too.

Playing Songs

You can play a single song, a whole list, or everything you have.

  • Play a Song
  • Play a Whole List
  • Pause and Resume
  • Skip to the Next Song
  • Go Back a Song or Start It Again
  • Return to Now Playing
  • What Now Playing Shows

The music plays on whatever you do in the MacMusic window or the menu bar.

To Play a Song

Highlight a song in any list, and click the centre button.

The song plays, and the Now Playing screen appears. When it ends, the next song in the list follows, and so on to the end of the list.

OR

Press Return or Enter.

To Play a Whole List

You can play everything an item leads to (an artist, an album, a genre or a playlist) without opening it.

Highlight the item and click the bottom of the click wheel.

Every song it leads to plays, from the first.

OR

Press the Space bar.

If Shuffle is on in Settings, the songs play in a random order.

To Pause and Resume

On Now Playing, click the bottom of the click wheel.

The music pauses, and the play icon changes to two bars.

Click it again to carry on.

OR

Press the Space bar.

On a list, this plays the highlighted item instead; use it on Now Playing to pause.

To Skip to the Next Song

Click the right side of the click wheel.

The next song in the list starts. After the last song, Next goes back to the first.

OR

Press the Right Arrow key.

Next works from any screen while music is playing.

To Go Back a Song or Start It Again

Click the left side of the click wheel.

If more than three seconds of the song have played, it starts again from the beginning. Otherwise, the song before it plays.

OR

Press the Left Arrow key.

To Return to Now Playing

While a song is playing or paused, Now Playing is the last item in the main menu.

Click MENU until you reach the main menu, then choose Now Playing.

When the music stops, the item goes away again.

What Now Playing Shows

  • where the song is in the list (“2 of 3”)
  • its title, artist and album
  • a bar showing how much has played
  • the time played, and the time left
  • at the top right, the Shuffle and Repeat icons, when they’re on

Shuffling

Shuffle plays songs in a random order, like a radio station that only plays your records.

  • Shuffle Every Song
  • Shuffle a List
  • Shuffle by Albums

Audiobooks are never shuffled.

To Shuffle Every Song

Choose Shuffle Songs from the main menu.

Every song in your library plays, in a random order, and Now Playing appears.

To Shuffle a List

Choose Settings from the main menu.

Highlight Shuffle, and click the centre button until it says Songs.

Each click shows the next setting: Off, Songs, Albums.

Play a list.

The song you chose plays first; the rest of the list follows in a random order.

The setting stays until you change it.

To Shuffle by Albums

In Settings, set Shuffle to Albums.

Play a list.

Its albums play in a random order, each one’s songs in track order, as their records would.

Moving Through a Song

While a song plays, the Now Playing screen lets you skip ahead, go back, or jump to any place in it, and set how loud it is.

  • Fast-Forward or Rewind
  • Use the Scrubber
  • Change the Volume

The music keeps playing while you do any of these.

To Fast-Forward or Rewind

Position the pointer on the right side of the click wheel and hold down the mouse button. To rewind, use the left side.

After half a second the play icon changes to two arrows, and the song moves on at four times its normal speed; after two seconds, sixteen times.

Release the mouse button.

The song carries on from the new place.

OR

Hold down Option and the Right Arrow or Left Arrow key.

To Use the Scrubber

On Now Playing, click the centre button.

A diamond, the scrubber, appears on the progress bar.

Turn the click wheel.

One full turn moves through the whole song. When you stop turning, the song jumps to the new place.

The scrubber goes away by itself after five seconds.

To Change the Volume

On Now Playing, turn the click wheel: clockwise for louder, anticlockwise for softer.

The volume bar takes the place of the progress bar. One full turn goes from the softest setting to the loudest.

OR

Press the Down Arrow key for louder, the Up Arrow key for softer, one step at a time.

MacMusic remembers the volume for next time.

Headphones, Speakers and Tape

The sound output port on the back of your Macintosh lets you listen through headphones, play your music through a speaker or a stereo, or record it onto tape.

  • Listen Through Headphones
  • Play Through a Speaker or Stereo
  • Record Onto Tape

The Macintosh has one sound channel, so its sound, and MacMusic’s, is mono. While something is plugged into the port, the built-in speaker is silent. For more about the port, see the Macintosh Owner’s Guide.

To Listen Through Headphones

On Now Playing, turn the click wheel anticlockwise to the softest volume.

Plug the headphones into the sound output port, and put them on.

Play a song, and turn the click wheel clockwise, a step at a time, to a comfortable level.

The port takes a mono 3.5 mm plug. Stereo headphones need an adaptor to hear the music in both ears.

To Play Through a Speaker or Stereo

Use an amplified speaker, or a stereo’s auxiliary or line input; not a microphone or record-player input.

Turn the speaker’s volume all the way down, and turn MacMusic’s down too.

Connect the cable: a mono 3.5 mm plug at the Macintosh, and an adaptor if the inputs are stereo, so the sound comes from both sides.

Play a song. Bring up the speaker’s volume to a comfortable level, then set MacMusic’s to suit.

The sound is usually clearest with MacMusic fairly loud and the speaker turned lower.

To Record Onto Tape

Connect the Macintosh to the tape recorder’s auxiliary or line input, not its microphone input, which would distort.

Set the recorder to record, and pause it.

Play the song. Set MacMusic’s volume, then the recorder’s recording level, so that the meters stay just short of the red in the loudest parts.

Choose the song again from its list to start it from the beginning, and release the recorder’s pause.

MacMusic’s volume has eight steps, so make fine adjustments on the recorder.

Settings

Settings, in the main menu, holds MacMusic’s preferences, and a few facts about your library. Legal shows MacMusic’s legal statement.

  • Change Shuffle or Repeat
  • See About
  • Reset All Settings

MacMusic remembers your settings from one time to the next.

To Change Shuffle or Repeat

Choose Settings from the main menu.

Highlight Shuffle or Repeat, and click the centre button.

Each click shows the next setting beside the item.

Shuffle: Off, Songs or Albums (see “Shuffling”).

Repeat: Off; One, the same song again and again; or All, back to the start of the list when it ends.

With Repeat One, Next and Previous still go to another song.

To See About

Choose About from Settings.

You see how many songs your library holds, out of the 500 it can; how much room is left on the library’s disk (Available); and MacMusic’s version.

To Reset All Settings

Choose Reset All Settings from Settings.

Cancel is highlighted, so a stray click does no harm.

Highlight Reset, and click the centre button.

Shuffle and Repeat go back to Off. The volume stays as it is.

Ratings, Backlight and the Clock

A few finishing touches: stars for your favourite songs, a backlight for show, and a clock.

  • Rate a Song
  • Turn the Backlight On or Off
  • See the Clock

To Rate a Song

On Now Playing, click the centre button twice.

The first click shows the scrubber; the second, the song’s rating.

Turn the click wheel to give the song from one to five stars.

The rating is saved with the song. After five seconds, the progress bar comes back.

MacMusic keeps ratings, but doesn’t use them for anything yet.

To Turn the Backlight On or Off

Choose Backlight from the main menu.

MacMusic’s screen turns light on dark. Choose Backlight again to turn it back.

It’s just for show, and it’s off again the next time you start MacMusic.

To See the Clock

Choose Extras from the main menu, and then Clock.

The Clock shows the time and date from your Macintosh’s own clock, and keeps up to the minute.

To set the time, use the Alarm Clock or the Control Panel desk accessory; see the Macintosh Owner’s Guide.

Adding Songs

Songs come into your library by importing them. MacMusic plays WAV sound files of one particular kind: mono, 22,050 samples a second, 8 bits. To make them from your own music, see MacMusic Utilities.

  • Import a Song
  • Import From a Floppy Disk or Another Hard Disk
  • Keep the Original
  • If the Song Is Already There
  • If the File Isn’t the Right Kind
  • If the Library Is Full
  • If There Isn’t Room on the Disk

A library can hold as many as 500 songs.

To Import a Song

Choose Import Song from the File menu.

Select a WAV file, and click Open.

If the file is on the same disk as your library, MacMusic says it will move the file into the library.

Click OK.

A dialog box shows the song’s details, filled in from the file where MacMusic can.

Type or change any of the details, and click Import.

Each detail can be up to 63 characters long.

To Import From a Floppy Disk or Another Hard Disk

MacMusic first asks: “Importing from another disk will take some time. Do you still want to import?”

Click Import.

When MacMusic says the file will be moved, click OK, or Just Copy to keep the original.

OK copies the song into your library, then removes the original from the other disk.

If the disk is locked, the original can’t be removed, and MacMusic tells you it has been left in place.

To Keep the Original

A file is normally moved into your library, so that it isn’t left in two places.

When MacMusic says the file will be moved, click Just Copy.

Your library gets a copy, and the original stays where it was. Copying takes longer than moving.

If the Song Is Already There

MacMusic recognises a song by its sound, even under another name. If you import one that’s already in your library, it tells you, and names the song.

Click Cancel to stop, or Import Anyway to have it twice.

If the File Isn’t the Right Kind

If the file isn’t a WAV file, MacMusic says so. If it’s a WAV file of another kind, it says what the file is, and what’s needed: “Only mono, 22050 Hz, 8-bit PCM WAV files can be imported.”

Click OK.

To prepare files MacMusic can play, see MacMusic Utilities.

If the Library Is Full

When your library holds 500 songs, MacMusic says “The MacMusic Library is full (500 songs). Export a song to make room.”

Click OK.

To make room, export songs you no longer want (see “Changing and Removing Songs”), or start another library.

If There Isn’t Room on the Disk

Music takes room: about 1.3 MB for each minute. If the library’s disk is too full for a song, MacMusic says so.

Click OK, make room on the disk, and import the song again.

Settings, About shows how much room is left.

Changing and Removing Songs

You can change any of a song’s details at any time, and remove a song you no longer want.

  • Change a Song’s Details
  • Export (Remove) a Song
  • Bring an Exported Song Back

Taking a song off a playlist doesn’t remove it from your library; see “Working With Playlists.”

To Change a Song’s Details

Highlight the song in a list, or play it, so that it’s on Now Playing.

Choose Edit Song from the Edit menu.

You see the same details as when you imported it.

Make your changes, and click Save.

The song is filed under its new details, and you stay on the same screen.

To Export (Remove) a Song

To remove a song, you export it: MacMusic gives it back as a WAV file, just as you imported it, and takes it out of your library and every playlist.

Highlight the song, and choose Export Song from the File menu.

Choose where to save the file (anywhere outside the library folder), and click Save.

If you don’t want the file, drag it to the Wastebasket.

To Bring an Exported Song Back

Import the exported file again.

MacMusic fills in the song’s details from the file where it can, as it did the first time.

The song comes back without its rating, and isn’t on any playlists; add it to them again.

Working With Playlists

A playlist lists songs from your library in the order you choose. You can have as many as ten.

  • Make a Playlist
  • Add a Song
  • Add a Song Again
  • Remove a Song From a Playlist
  • Rename a Playlist
  • Delete a Playlist
  • Playlists in the Finder

The commands for playlists are in the Playlists menu, in the menu bar.

To Make a Playlist

Choose New Playlist from the Playlists menu.

Type a name, and click Create.

The name can be up to 31 characters. The playlist appears under Playlists in the Music screen, and in the Playlists menu as Add to “name.”

To Add a Song

Highlight a song in any list.

Choose Add to “name” from the Playlists menu.

The song goes at the end of the playlist.

The Add to commands are dimmed unless a song is highlighted.

To Add a Song Again

A song can be on a playlist more than once. If it’s already there, MacMusic asks first.

Click Add Again to add it once more, or Cancel.

To Remove a Song From a Playlist

Open the playlist (Music, then Playlists, then the playlist), and highlight the song.

Choose Remove from Playlist from the Playlists menu.

The song comes off the playlist, but stays in your library.

To Rename a Playlist

Highlight the playlist in the Playlists list, or open it.

Choose Rename “name” from the Playlists menu.

Type the new name, and click Rename.

To Delete a Playlist

Highlight the playlist, or open it.

Choose Delete “name” from the Playlists menu.

MacMusic asks you to confirm.

Click Delete.

The playlist goes; its songs stay in your library.

Playlists in the Finder

Each playlist is a text file in the Playlists folder inside your library, listing its songs, one a line.

You can open one with TeachText to read it, but change playlists only with MacMusic.

Using More Than One Library

You might keep one library on your hard disk and another on a different disk. MacMusic uses one library at a time, and remembers the last five you’ve used.

  • Create Another Library
  • Open an Existing Library
  • Switch to a Library You’ve Used Before
  • Rescan a Library
  • See Library Info
  • If MacMusic Can’t Find Your Library
  • Carry On Without a Library

The library in use is checked in the File menu.

To Create Another Library

Choose Choose Library from the File menu.

MacMusic asks whether to use an existing library or create a new one.

Click Create New Library. Choose where to put it, name it, and click Save.

MacMusic switches to the new, empty library.

If you choose a folder that’s already a library, MacMusic offers to use it instead.

To Open an Existing Library

Choose Choose Library from the File menu, and click Use Existing Library.

Open the library’s folder, select its MacMusic Library Info file, or any of its songs, and click Open.

MacMusic reads the library, and shows the main menu.

To Switch to a Library You’ve Used Before

The libraries you’ve used are listed in the File menu.

Choose one from the File menu.

A library whose disk isn’t there is dimmed. Insert the disk, or switch on the hard disk, and try again.

To Rescan a Library

MacMusic reads your library each time it starts. To have it read the library again:

Choose Rescan Library from the File menu.

A large library takes a while: a minute and a half or so for 500 songs.

To See Library Info

Choose Library Info from the File menu.

You see how many songs and playlists the library has, how much room its songs take, how much is free on its disk, and how many files couldn’t be read.

Click OK.

If MacMusic Can’t Find Your Library

If the library’s disk isn’t there when MacMusic starts (a floppy not inserted, say, or a hard disk not switched on), MacMusic says so.

Insert the disk or switch it on, and click Find Existing; or click Create New.

To Carry On Without a Library

When MacMusic asks for a library, click Not Now.

MacMusic starts with nothing in it. You can choose a library at any time with Choose Library in the File menu.

MacMusic and Your Other Applications

MacMusic plays on while you do other things. Under MultiFinder you can work in another application with the music playing, and desk accessories can be used at any time.

  • Keep Playing While You Work
  • Use Desk Accessories
  • See How Much Memory MacMusic Uses
  • Change MacMusic’s Memory Size

If another application keeps the Macintosh completely busy for more than about six seconds, you may hear a moment of music repeat, then a pause; the music then carries on by itself.

To Keep Playing While You Work

Under MultiFinder, start a song, then open another application or click in one of its windows.

The music plays on. Click in the MacMusic window to come back to it.

Without MultiFinder, quitting MacMusic to open another application stops the music.

To Use Desk Accessories

Choose a desk accessory from the Apple menu.

The music plays on while you use it.

Click in the MacMusic window to bring it to the front again.

To See How Much Memory MacMusic Uses

Under MultiFinder, click the desktop to make the Finder active, and choose About the Finder from the Apple menu.

A bar shows how much memory each open application has. MacMusic asks for 640K.

To Change MacMusic’s Memory Size

MacMusic works best with 640K of memory, and needs at least 512K.

Quit MacMusic. In the Finder, select its icon and choose Get Info from the File menu.

Type a new size in the Application Memory Size box, and close the window.
