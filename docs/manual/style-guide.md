# MacMusic manual: style guide

Phase 1 of `docs/plans/manual-1987.md`. How the MacMusic manual will
read and look, taken from four of Apple's Macintosh application manuals
and their packaging. The references themselves stay outside the
repository (they are Apple's copyright). This guide describes them,
quoting only short phrases, and every example sentence written for
MacMusic is new.

## 1. The sources

| Code | Manual | Year | Pages | Studied |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MP | MacPaint | 1984 | 36 | all |
| MW | MacWrite | 1985 | 176 | front matter, all chapter openers, the tutorial, task, reference, glossary and index pages, the 4.6 update insert |
| MJ | MacProject | 1984 | 142 | front matter, openers, tutorial, reference, glossary, index, covers, flap |
| MD | MacDraw | 1984 | 123 | front matter, openers, tutorial, task and reference pages, covers |

Plus photographs of the MacDraw, MacProject, Macintosh Pascal and
MacTerminal packages (§13).

Citations such as "MW 13" are the PDF page. Eleven MacWrite pages
couldn't be decoded from the scan (PDF 143, 151, 156, 160-163, 165,
173-175); they fall in the index and the update insert, which the
readable pages cover well. The page size can only be estimated from the
scans' proportions (§6.1).

## 2. Three styles, and which to follow

The four manuals show three related designs:

- **A. MacPaint (1984):** set entirely in Helvetica. The heading sits in
  a narrow outer margin column; topics are pages of three or four narrow
  columns with small pictures. Red marks show actions (click, drag,
  press, release). It's a "book of hints" (MP 4), short, with no glossary
  or index.
- **B. Apple's 1984-85 house design (MacWrite, MacProject, MacDraw):** text
  in Apple's condensed Garamond, headings in bold Helvetica, and a second
  colour (red-orange) for margin headings and lesson numbers. The structure
  is learning, using, reference, glossary, index. Three of the four
  manuals follow it, nearly identically.
- **C. The MacWrite 4.6 update (about 1986, MW 166-172):** a newer design.
  Section heads are in a bold geometric sans under a double rule, the
  "Important" notes are boxed between rules, and the body is in an
  old-style serif.

**This guide follows B**, the design the references share. It takes two
things from the others: A's red action marks for mouse actions (§8.4),
and C's ruled "Important" note (§7.6), which is the most 1986-87 thing
the references show.

## 3. Voice

What the manuals sound like, with a MacMusic example of each.

- **Talk to the reader, as "you"; Apple is "we".** The tone is warm and
  plain, like a helpful colleague, never a specification (MW 5, MJ 5).
  *MacMusic plays the music stored on your disk, much as a pocket music
  player would.*
- **Short sentences, one idea each.** Explain why before how, especially
  at the start of a chapter (MJ 5: the barbecue as project management).
- **Meet the reader where they are.** Say what they need to know already,
  and send them to the *Macintosh Owner's Guide* for the basics (MW 5,
  MD 4, MP 4). Offer more than one way in, including "just try it" (MD 4).
  *If you'd rather start playing right away, turn to "A Tour of MacMusic."*
- **Use everyday comparisons for new ideas** (MJ 5-7). *A playlist is like
  a mix tape: the songs stay where they are, and the playlist just lists
  them in the order you choose.*
- **Allow a little humour, gently and rarely:** one light remark in a few
  pages (MW 12, the "window" aside; MW 16, Undo; MD 2, the credits).
  *Holding Next is the fastest way to find the chorus.*
- **Describe the senses and the waiting:** what you'll see and hear, and
  how long it takes (MJ 14: a soft hum, "in a few seconds"; MW 11: the
  wristwatch). *You'll see the wristwatch for a moment while MacMusic
  reads your library.*
- **After each action, say what happens.** The result goes in the next
  sentence, in plain text (MW 11: the menu bar and icons appear).
- **End lessons with an invitation** to explore, and say where to go next
  (MW 45 "What Now?", MJ 28 "Experimenting").
- **State limits plainly, as figures** (MW 111, MJ 100). *A library can
  hold as many as 500 songs.*

## 4. Vocabulary

Use the Macintosh terms the manuals use, and define each the first time
in bold:

- **Mouse actions:**
  - **click:** position the pointer, then press and quickly release the
    button (MW 11);
  - **press:** hold the button down;
  - **drag:** press, move, release (MP 7);
  - **double-click**;
  - **Shift-click**.
- **Menus:** you **choose** a command **from** a menu (MW 11); a command
  that can't be used is **dimmed** (MW 145).
- **The screen:** **pointer**, **icon**, **window**, **title bar**,
  **close box**, **scroll bar**, **size box**, **menu bar**, **desktop**,
  **Finder**, **dialog box**, **alert box**, **button**, **desk
  accessory**, **disk**, **startup disk**.
- **Keys:** "press" means a light tap unless the text says "press and
  hold" (MW 13). Keys are named with capitals: the Return key, the
  Command key, the Option key.
- **Commands with "..."** keep the ellipsis when named as headings (MJ 118,
  "Calendar..."), but not in running text.
- **Never "iPod" (owner's rule, 2026-10-08).** The program is
  **MacMusic** throughout: the **MacMusic window**, **MacMusic's screen**
  (the small display in the window), the **click wheel**, the **main
  menu**, **Now Playing**. Comparisons speak of "a pocket music player",
  never a brand. Two things can't follow the rule, and the text doesn't
  draw attention to them:
  - the pictures, where MacMusic's own screen shows "iPod" as the main
    menu's title, and some older shots show the library's earlier name;
    the text simply calls it the main menu;
  - the legal statement, quoted as it is.
- **MacMusic's own terms,** defined in the glossary, with bold at first
  use: **MacMusic Library**, **song**, **playlist**, **click wheel**,
  **Now Playing**, **audiobook**, **library marker** ("MacMusic Library
  Info"), **Shuffle**, **Repeat**, **scrubber**, **rating**.

Naming things in text:
- **commands and menu names** in plain text, as in MW: "Choose Import Song
  from the File menu". In step lines they're part of the bold step (§7.1).
- **the Mac's own text** (button names, messages) in quotation marks
  where it's quoted in full.
- **book titles** in italics (*Macintosh Owner's Guide*, *MacMusic User's Guide*).
- **chapter cross-references** as Chapter 2, "Managing Your Music Library".

## 5. The book's structure

From MW 4, MJ 3-14 and MD 4-5:

1. **Front cover** (§12).
2. **Inside front cover:** the copyright page (§12.3).
3. **Title page:** the product name and the four-column grid rules
   (MW 3, MD 3).
4. **Contents:** one book-wide contents page (MW 4, MD 5).
5. **About This Manual:** what the program is for, what each chapter
   does, what the reader should know already, and a word to people coming
   from a sister product (MW 5, MD 4).
6. **Chapters,** each opening with:
   - an opener spread (§8.6);
   - its own contents page (MW 2 of each chapter; MJ 12, 36);
   - an Introduction.

   The pattern is:
   - **Chapter 1, Learning:** a hands-on tutorial in numbered lessons,
     ending with "What Now?" (MW, MJ, MD). MacProject puts an orientation
     before it, explaining the ideas in everyday terms (MJ 3-9).
   - **Chapter 2, Using:** task pages, one task per column, grouped by
     topic (MW 48-100, MD 32).
   - **Chapter 3, Reference:** concepts, then every menu and command,
     dialogs, limits and preset values (MW 104-130, MJ 100-118,
     MD 66-101).
7. **Glossary** (MW 136, MJ 126).
8. **Index** (MW 145, MJ 134).
9. **Inside back cover** and **back cover** (§12.4).

For MacMusic, the plan's outline (`docs/plans/manual-1987.md`) fits this
pattern, with three adjustments:
- an orientation section, "What Is MacMusic?";
- Chapter 3 as the task chapter;
- the menu reference and messages as the reference chapter.

The outline (Phase 3) will be set in this shape.

## 6. Page design

### 6.1 Size and binding

- **Page size:** the scans' proportions (about 0.83, width to height)
  point to roughly **7½ × 9 inches**, the usual size of Apple's manuals
  of the time. This is to be confirmed by measuring a physical copy if
  one is to hand.
- **Binding:** wire-O for MacProject and MacDraw (holes show in every
  scan, MJ, MD), with a wraparound end flap whose spine carries the logo
  and the title set vertically (MJ 142). MacPaint and MacWrite have
  ordinary bound spines.

### 6.2 The grid

- A **margin column** about a quarter of the text width, **always on the
  left**, on left-hand and right-hand pages alike (MW 5, MJ 5, MJ 14,
  MD 16). (MacPaint alone keeps its heading column on the page's outer
  side, MP 4-7; corrected 2026-10-08, Phase 4.)
- A **thin vertical rule** at its inner edge, running the height of the
  text area (MW 5, MJ 5).
- **One wide text column** beside it.
- On task pages, the whole width is divided into **four equal columns**
  with rules between (MW 56, 60), the first being the margin column.
- The **title page** shows the four-column rules alone (MW 3, MD 3).

### 6.3 Type

| Element | Face | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Body text | condensed Garamond, about 12/15 | ragged right (MW 5). For MacMusic: EB Garamond at normal width, 13/16 (P3, chosen over a version narrowed to 86% as it reads better, 2026-10-08) |
| Margin headings (sections) | bold Helvetica, about 14 pt, in the second colour | MW 5, MJ 5 |
| Lesson headings | bold Helvetica, about 11 pt, beside a numbered red square | MW 13, MJ 14 |
| Step lines | bold Helvetica, about 11 pt, after a black square ■ | MW 11 |
| Task headings ("To ...") | bold Helvetica, about 11 pt, black | MW 56, MD 32 |
| Glossary terms, reference command heads | bold Helvetica | MW 119, 136 |
| Supplementary notes | Garamond in light grey | MW 45, 56 |
| Running foot | Garamond: the chapter name on left-hand pages, the section name on right-hand ones | MW 12-13 |
| Index | Garamond, two columns; letter heads in bold Helvetica, in the second colour | MW 145, MJ 134 |
| Screen text in figures | the Mac's own screen fonts (real screen images) | |

MacProject sets its running foot in letterspaced Garamond small capitals
(MJ 5); MacWrite and MacDraw use upper and lower case. This guide uses
MacWrite's.

### 6.4 Colour

One colour besides black, a warm **red-orange**, used for:
- margin headings;
- lesson number squares;
- callout leader lines;
- action marks;
- index letters;
- the "Contents" heading.

It's never used for body text (MW throughout, MJ throughout). MacDraw's
figure leaders are black (MD 16), so red leaders are optional.

### 6.5 Numbering

- **Pages:** numbered from the start of Chapter 1, with the front matter
  in lower-case roman (MW 4-5: ii, iii). The number sits at the foot's
  outer edge.
- **Chapters:** "Chapter 1".
- **Lessons:** numbered within the tutorial chapter.

## 7. Instructions

### 7.1 Steps

- Each step is a black square ■ and a **bold Helvetica imperative
  sentence** (MW 11, MJ 14). *■ Choose Import Song from the File menu.*
- The result follows in Garamond, as plain sentences. *The Import Song
  dialog box appears, listing the WAV files on your disk.*
- A step names the menu after the command: "Choose X from the Y menu."

### 7.2 Introducing steps

Use a short bold run-in phrase ending with a colon or a full stop:
"Here's how." (MJ 28), "To see how ...:" (MP 9).

### 7.3 Techniques, defined in passing

The first time a technique is used, define it with a bold run-in term
and a colon (MW 11). *To drag: Position the pointer on the click wheel,
press and hold the mouse button, and move the pointer round the wheel.*

### 7.4 Lessons

- A tutorial chapter is a series of **numbered lessons**: a red square
  with a white numeral and a bold heading in the margin (MW 13, MJ 28).
- Each lesson is a few steps with their results.
- The last lesson is an invitation to experiment (MJ 28), and the chapter
  ends with "What Now?" (MW 45).

### 7.5 Lists

- Explanatory lists use **open squares** ☐ (MW 9, MJ 100).
- Steps use **black squares** ■ (§7.1).
- Task-page steps use small **grey squares** (MW 56, MD 32).

### 7.6 Notes

- **Grey text:** a supplementary remark after a step is set in light grey
  (MW 45, 56).
- **Important:** for anything that could lose work, use C's style, the
  word **Important** in the margin and the note in bold between a rule
  above and a rule below (MW 166, 172). Use it sparingly: two or three in
  the book.

### 7.7 Text to type

Text the reader types is set in Helvetica, indented, between dotted
rules like lined paper (MW 13).

## 8. Figures

### 8.1 Screen images

- **Real screens:** actual 1-bit screen images, full windows or crops,
  printed at a modest size, with a light grey tone in the B manuals
  (MW 11, 16).
- **Crops:** a partial screen ends in a torn, zigzag edge (MW 16).
- **Dialog and alert boxes:** shown whole, in black (MJ 118, MD 82).

### 8.2 Callouts

- The parts of a window are labelled in the margin, with **thin leader
  lines**, horizontal then angled, to each part (MW 12, MJ 14).
- The label names the part in bold and says what it does, in one line.
  *The **close box** closes the window.*
- Dialogs can be labelled on both sides (MW 122).

### 8.3 Where figures go

- In the text column, after the step they show (MW 11).
- On task pages, at the column foot (MW 56, MD 32).

### 8.4 Action marks (from MacPaint)

- Red marks show mouse actions in pictures:
  - a small "click" mark;
  - a dashed L with an arrowhead for "drag";
  - "Press" and "Release" labels at the start and end of a drag (MP 7-8).
- Drag paths are red dashed lines with arrowheads (MD 32).
- For MacMusic, a red dashed arc round the click wheel can show a turn.

### 8.5 Pictures of things

- **Keycaps** are drawn as small key outlines with the key's name
  (MP 34, MW 136): Return, Option, Command, Delete.
- **Buttons** (OK, Cancel) and **icons** are shown beside the glossary
  terms they name (MW 136).

### 8.6 Chapter openers

- A **two-page spread** (MW 6-7, MD 7-8), or a right-hand page (MP 5, 11).
- A large image across it, bleeding off the edges. The B manuals use
  either a black-and-white studio photograph of something from everyday
  life (a hand pointing at a mouse, MW 6-7; dinner preparation, MJ 3) or
  the program's own artwork in grey (MD 7-8). MacPaint uses its own
  1-bit illustrations (MP 5).
- "Chapter n" and the chapter title in Garamond, set in the right-hand
  page's margin column at the top (MW 7, MD 8).

### 8.7 Decorative photographs

The B manuals scatter a few studio photographs of props through the task
chapter, bleeding off a page edge: a coffee cup and saucer, for example
(MW 60).

## 9. Task pages (the "Using" chapter)

- The first column holds:
  - the topic heading, in the second colour ("Selecting", "Removing");
  - a short Garamond introduction, which may have a ☐ list of what the
    page covers (MW 56, 60).
- Each further column is **one task**:
  - a bold heading, "To ...";
  - grey-square steps;
  - "OR" between alternatives;
  - grey notes;
  - a small screen figure at the foot.
- The task chapter has its own contents page listing every task (MW 48,
  MJ 36), so a reader can find a task in seconds.

## 10. Reference pages

- **Concepts first** (MW 111, MJ 100): what the program's objects are,
  each under a red-square subheading, with ☐ lists of their parts and
  their limits as figures.
- **Then each menu:**
  - a red-square subheading, "File Menu";
  - a picture of the menu pulled down;
  - each command as a bold heading, with its ellipsis ("Import Song...");
  - a Garamond paragraph saying what the command does, when it's dimmed,
    and what dialog appears, with the dialog shown (MW 119-122,
    MD 82).
- **Desk accessories** that are useful with the program get a short
  list (MW 119). For MacMusic, that's the Alarm Clock and the Control
  Panel, for the volume and the time.
- **End with** the preset values and the specifications, in a two-column
  table (MW's contents, MW 4, lists them; MW 172 shows C's table).

## 11. Glossary and index

- **Glossary:**
  - the heading in grey bold Helvetica in the margin (MW 136);
  - entries alphabetical, each a bold Helvetica term (lower case unless
    a name) on its own line, then a one- or two-sentence Garamond
    definition;
  - small pictures in the margin beside some terms: a button, a keycap,
    the Apple menu (MW 136, MJ 126).
- **Index:**
  - two Garamond columns, under bold Helvetica letter heads in the second
    colour, with empty letters kept (MJ 134 shows "J");
  - sub-entries indented;
  - page ranges with hyphens;
  - "See" and "See also" in italics (MW 145, MJ 134).

## 12. Covers and front matter

### 12.1 Front cover (MP 1, MW 1, MJ 1, MD 1)

- A white or cream ground.
- The product name in Garamond, near the top. MacWrite and MacPaint have
  "Macintosh" in front of it (white on a black box, on MacWrite).
- The logo at the top left. *Not for MacMusic (MM1); see §14.*
- The main image: a photograph of the program's screen (tinted pale blue
  in print), with the mouse cable curving down from it to a mouse under a
  hand in a white shirt cuff.
- MacWrite adds a short blurb in condensed Helvetica.

### 12.2 Title page (MW 3, MD 3)

The product name in Garamond at the top left, and the four-column grid
rules down the page. The logo in red sits at the top right. *For
MacMusic, the logo is replaced (see §14).*

### 12.3 Copyright page (MW 2, MJ 2, MD 2)

- Two columns in Garamond.
- **Left:** a bold "Copyright" heading, the licence wording, the
  copyright line with the publisher's address, the trademark lines, the
  credits ("Written by ...", "Program by ...", a playful extra line),
  "Simultaneously published in ..." and the reorder number.
- **Right:** the limited warranty, its disclaimers in bold capitals.

### 12.4 Back cover (MP 36, MJ 140, MD 123)

- Almost empty.
- The publisher's name in heavy bold Helvetica, with its address in
  Garamond, at the top left (B) or the bottom left (A).
- The logo at the bottom left, and a small part number at the bottom
  right.

## 13. Packaging (for later, if wanted)

From the photographs:
- **The box:**
  - a white slip-case "album" box the size of the manual, its front
    repeating the cover;
  - the spine with the name set vertically;
  - a grey back with a paragraph of what the product does for you, a
    round-bulleted list of bold claims, two screen shots and the small
    legal lines;
  - inside, a moulded tray holding the disks and a "Guided Tour" disk
    and cassette, under a lid printed with a coloured line drawing of the
    Macintosh.
- **Disk labels:** speckled white, with the name in Garamond, a 1-bit
  pixel icon of the product, and a version and part number on the back.
- **Inserts:** a letter from the product manager, and a registration
  card.

MacMusic's packaging could follow this later (a disk label, a box). It
isn't part of the manual plan.

## 14. Adapting it for MacMusic (MM1, MM2)

- **The own-use edition (MM1a, 2026-10-08)** makes two exceptions,
  kept outside the repository: the cover's hand, mouse and cable, from
  MacDraw's cover, and the rainbow logo (cover, title page, back
  cover). The points below hold for everything else, and for the plain
  edition.
- **No Apple identity:**
  - no Apple logo anywhere;
  - no "Apple Computer, Inc." as publisher, and no Apple part numbers;
  - "Macintosh" never as a brand box on the cover (it would read as
    Apple's).
- **MacMusic's own mark:** where the logo stood (cover, title page, back
  cover), MacMusic's own 32 × 32 icon from Fix 1.
- **The copyright page** carries:
  - © 2026 Jack McAteer;
  - the trademark acknowledgements (Apple, Macintosh, Mac and iPod belong
    to their owners) and MacMusic's not-affiliated statement;
  - "Written by ..." and "Program by ..." credits as the owner wishes;
  - in place of a warranty, the legal statement's terms, or a pointer to
    the licence.
- **The Macintosh Plus only** (MU1): nothing about modern computers,
  converting songs, BlueSCSI or the project's tools; the companion book
  *MacMusic Utilities* is named where a reader needs it (MU2), in
  italics, with a chapter name where useful (MU5, provisional): see
  "Converting Songs" in *MacMusic Utilities*. The hard disk is "an Apple
  Hard Disk 20SC or other SCSI hard disk" (MU4); volumes up to 2 GB are
  allowed.
- **Requirements stated as they are** (MM2): a Mac Plus with
  System 6.0.7, WAV songs at 22,050 Hz, 8-bit and mono, in the period
  voice. *To use MacMusic, you need a Macintosh Plus with System 6.0.7.*
- **No warranty, reorder number or part number** that implies a real
  1980s product line. A "MacMusic 1.0" edition line takes their place.

## 15. Decisions for the owner

**Decided 2026-10-08:** SG1-SG8 all as recommended (the owner will
revise by hand later).

**Added 2026-10-08, after the Chapter 1 draft:**
- **SG9, spelling:** British throughout (centre, favourite, colour,
  initialise, anticlockwise), except the Mac's own words, quoted as they
  appear (the Finder's Initialize button).
- **SG10, the openers:** each chapter's opener spread is MacMusic's own
  screen in light grey, bleeding off both pages, with the chapter's name
  in a white panel in the right-hand page's margin column (as Chapter 1).
  Each chapter uses a different screen, to suit it: Chapter 1 the main
  menu; Chapter 2 Now Playing, say; Chapter 3 a Settings or menu
  screen.


- **SG1, the base design:** B (Garamond and Helvetica, red-orange second
  colour), with MacPaint's action marks and the 1986 "Important" note.
  *Recommended.* The alternative is A, MacPaint's all-Helvetica hints
  book: shorter and lighter, but without a glossary or index.
- **SG2, the page size:** 7½ × 9 inches, from the scans. If you have one
  of these manuals to hand, a measurement would settle it.
- **SG3, the binding look:** wire-O, with a printed spine flap (MacProject
  and MacDraw), or a plain bound spine (MacWrite). It affects only the
  PDF's look (a spine strip and flap page). *Recommended: wire-O with the
  flap.*
- **SG4, the cover image:**
  - **(a)** a real photograph: your Mac Plus showing MacMusic, the mouse
    under a hand, on white. That's the references' own approach, and the
    most convincing.
  - **(b)** a drawn line illustration in the same composition.
  - **(c)** the screen image alone.

  *Recommended: (a) if you can take the photograph; else (b).*
- **SG5, the chapter openers:**
  - **(a)** studio photographs of everyday things, which you'd take
    (headphones on a desk, a stack of floppy disks, a cassette);
  - **(b)** large 1-bit MacPaint-style artwork in grey, drawn for
    MacMusic, as MD and MP do;
  - **(c)** a mix.

  *Recommended: (b), as it needs no photography and is fully in period;
  (a) where you can supply photos.*
- **SG6, decorative props:** occasional photographs bleeding off the
  page, such as headphones or a cassette (§8.7), only if you supply
  them. *Recommended: optional.*
- **SG7, the running foot:** MacWrite's upper and lower case. MacProject's
  small capitals are the alternative. *Recommended: MacWrite's.*
- **SG8, colour:** the red-orange second colour, as all three B manuals
  use. The alternative is black only. *Recommended: the second colour.*
