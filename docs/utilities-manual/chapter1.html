From Your Music to MacMusic

MacMusic plays songs on the Macintosh Plus, from its own kind of sound file. The three MacMusic utilities run on your Mac, and between them take a song from wherever it is now to the Plus, and keep the Plus’s disks safe.

The MacMusic Converter Utility makes a song into a MacMusic WAV file. The MacMusic Transfer Utility puts MacMusic WAV files on a disk for the Plus, as a disk image that its BlueSCSI reads from its SD card. And the Macintosh Backup Utility keeps copies of the card on your Mac, so that if anything on it is ever lost, it can be put back.

What’s on the USB Drive

The USB drive in the box holds:

  • the three utilities, each as a zip file;
  • this manual and the MacMusic User’s Guide, to read on your Mac;
  • the MacMusic disk, for the Macintosh Plus;
  • the utilities’ source code and licences;
  • a Read Me.

PROVISIONAL: the drive’s contents are settled when it’s made (UB8).

What You Need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26, with a slot or reader for SD cards.
  • The Macintosh Plus, with System 6.0.7, MacMusic, and a BlueSCSI.
  • For YouTube, an internet connection.
1Installing the Utilities

Each utility comes as a zip file. Installing one takes a minute; the pictures show the MacMusic Converter Utility, and the others go the same way.

Plug in the USB drive, and open it in the Finder.

Double-click the utility’s zip file.

macOS expands it.

The utility appears beside the zip file.

Drag the utility onto Applications, in the sidebar of the Finder window.

It’s copied into your Applications folder.

Open the Applications folder, and double-click the utility to open it.

If macOS won’t open it the first time, saying it can’t check it, open System Settings, click Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway beside its name; then open it again. (The utilities are made for your own use, and aren’t registered with Apple.)

Do the same for the MacMusic Transfer Utility and the Macintosh Backup Utility.

2A Song’s Journey

This lesson follows one song from your Mac to the Plus, a step to each program, and says where each step is explained in full. Have a song of your own ready: an MP3, say.

Make it a MacMusic WAV: open the MacMusic Converter Utility, click Choose File, choose the song, click MacMusic WAV, and click Convert.

The WAV file is saved in the MacMusic Converter folder in your Music folder. (Chapter 2, Lesson 1.)

Put it on a disk for the Plus: open the MacMusic Transfer Utility, drag the WAV file onto its window, and click Build Transfer Image.

The disk image, HD60_512 Transfer.hda, is saved in the MacMusic Transfer folder in your Music folder. (Chapter 3, Lesson 1.)

Shut the Plus down with Shut Down in the Special menu, switch it off, and put its SD card in your Mac.

Back the card up: the Macintosh Backup Utility offers to when the card appears; click Back Up Now, then Back Up. (Chapter 4, Lesson 1.)

A backup before you change the card means nothing can be lost.

Drag the disk image onto the card, eject it, put it back in the BlueSCSI, and switch the Plus on.

The Transfer disk appears on the Plus’s desktop.

In MacMusic, choose Import Song from the File menu, and open the Transfer disk’s WAV Files folder. (MacMusic User’s Guide, Lesson 7.)

The song is in your library, ready to play.

What Now?

Each utility has a chapter of its own, with lessons, a task for everything it does, and a reference to every part of its window:

  • Chapter 2, “Converting Songs”: the MacMusic Converter Utility;
  • Chapter 3, “Moving Songs to the Macintosh”: the MacMusic Transfer Utility;
  • Chapter 4, “Backing Up Your Macintosh”: the Macintosh Backup Utility.

The appendixes list every message the utilities show, describe the files they make, and say what to do when something goes wrong.
