Introduction

The MacMusic Converter Utility makes your music into files that MacMusic can play. MacMusic plays one kind of sound file only: a WAV file that is mono, with 22,050 samples a second, 8 bits each (MacMusic User’s Guide, Appendix B). The Converter makes exactly that kind, from almost any sound file on your Mac, or from a YouTube video you own or have permission to save.

It runs on your Mac, not on the Macintosh Plus. Chapter 3 explains how to take what it makes to the Plus.

What it makes

Each time you convert a song, the Converter makes either or both of these:

  • A MacMusic WAV: mono, 22,050 Hz, 8-bit. It’s checked against MacMusic’s own rules before it’s kept, and it carries the song’s title, so MacMusic fills the title in for you when you import it.
  • An MP3: 192 kbps, stereo if the song is. It’s for listening on your Mac or elsewhere; it never goes to the Macintosh Plus.

Where songs come from

  • A sound file on your Mac: an MP3, AAC or M4A, WAV, FLAC, Opus and most other kinds.
  • A YouTube video: one at a time, by its address. The Converter doesn’t sign in to YouTube, so private, age-restricted and members-only videos can’t be saved. Use it only for videos you own or have permission to save.

Safe with your files

The Converter never changes the song you start from. It asks before it replaces anything. If you cancel, or quit while it works, nothing half-made is left behind, and every file is checked before it’s kept.

What it makes goes in a folder called MacMusic Converter, inside the Music folder in your home folder, unless you choose another.

1Converting a Song From a File

In this lesson you make a MacMusic WAV from an MP3 on your Mac. Any song you have will do; the pictures show “Good Luck, Babe!”.

Open the MacMusic Converter Utility.

It’s in the Applications folder; double-click its icon. Its window opens with Local File chosen at the top.

Click Choose File.

A dialog box opens, showing the folders on your Mac.

Find the song, select it, and click Open.

The window shows the song’s name and folder, and under them what it is: an MP3, at 44,100 samples a second, in stereo, three minutes 38 seconds long.

You can drag a song from the Finder onto the window instead.

Under Output, click MacMusic WAV.

The Title is filled in from the song’s own details, if it has any. This is the title MacMusic will show; type another if you like.

Leave File name empty, and the file is named after the title. Saves as shows the name it will have: Good Luck, Babe!.wav.

Click Convert.

At the foot of the window, the Converter says what it’s doing as it works: checking the file, analysing the sound, making the MacMusic WAV, and checking the result. A song of a few minutes takes a few seconds.

When it’s finished, Results shows the new file, its length and size, with a Show in Finder button to find it. (Lesson 2 pictures a result.)

2Converting a YouTube Video

Now you make a MacMusic WAV and an MP3 from a YouTube video. Use one you own or have permission to save; the pictures show a piece of music its channel offers free of copyright.

Click YouTube at the top of the window.

In your web browser, copy the video’s address. Then click in the box under YouTube Video, and choose Paste from the Edit menu.

Click Look Up.

The Converter asks YouTube about the video.

In a moment you see its title, its channel and its length.

If the address is of a video in a playlist, the playlist is ignored: only the one video is converted.

Under Output, click Both.

Type a shorter title: Chill Dreamy.

The title of a video is often long. MacMusic shows up to 63 characters of a title, and the WAV file’s name can be no more than 31, so a short title is best. Saves as shows the two files you’ll get.

Click Convert.

The Converter fetches the sound from YouTube first, then makes each file in turn.

Results shows what was done, and the files made. Click Show in Finder to see them in their folder.

This video was normalised by −2.4 dB, and its last three seconds of silence trimmed: those are choices under Sound, described in the tasks that follow.

Choosing a Song

The Converter works from one song at a time: a sound file on your Mac (Local File) or a YouTube video (YouTube). Choose which at the top of the window.

To Convert a Sound File

Click Local File at the top of the window.

Click Choose File, select the song, and click Open.

OR

Drag the song from the Finder onto the window.

The window shows what the song is. To choose a different one, click Choose Another.

To Convert a YouTube Video

Click YouTube at the top of the window.

Paste the video’s address into the box, and click Look Up (or press Return).

Its title, channel and length appear. Only one video is converted, even if the address is of a playlist.

Only for videos you own or have permission to save.

If a Video Can’t Be Used

The Converter says why, in place of the video’s details:

  • The video is private.
  • YouTube needs signing in to play it (it may be age-restricted or for members only). The Converter doesn’t sign in.
  • It isn’t available in your country.
  • It isn’t available: it may have been removed, or the address is wrong.
  • The address isn’t of one video (a playlist or a channel, perhaps), or isn’t a YouTube address.
  • YouTube couldn’t be reached: check the internet connection, and try again.

If it says YouTube has changed in a way it can’t handle, see Appendix C, “Troubleshooting.”

Choosing What to Make

A MacMusic WAV is the kind of file MacMusic plays. An MP3 is for listening anywhere else. The Converter makes either, or both from the one job.

To Make a MacMusic WAV, an MP3 or Both

Under Output, click MacMusic WAV, MP3 or Both.

Saves as lists the file, or files, you’ll get. The Converter remembers your choice for next time.

If the Song Is Already an MP3

Making a new MP3 from an MP3 re-encodes it, which loses a little quality. The window warns you when you choose MP3 or Both.

When you click Convert, it asks:

  • Re-encode makes the MP3 anyway.
  • WAV Only makes just the MacMusic WAV.
  • Cancel makes nothing.

Naming the Song

Each song has a title, which MacMusic shows, and a file name, which is what the file is called on your Mac and on the Macintosh Plus.

To Set the Title MacMusic Shows

Type it in the Title box.

It’s filled in for you from the song’s own details, or the video’s title. It goes inside the WAV file, and MacMusic puts it in the Title box when you import the song. MacMusic keeps up to 63 characters.

To Set the File Name

Type it in the File name box, without .wav or .mp3.

Left empty, the file is named after the title.

A MacMusic WAV’s name can be no more than 31 characters, with .wav (the Macintosh’s limit), so a long name is cut, at a word where it can be. Colons are left out, and letters the Macintosh can’t show are changed for the nearest it can. An MP3 keeps its whole name.

Saves as shows the names before you convert.

The Sound

Two choices under Sound change the music a little, and both start off. The Converter remembers them for next time.

To Even Out the Loudness

Turn on Normalise.

The song is made louder or quieter so that its loudest moment comes to a set level (−1.0 dBFS, just below the most a file can hold). Songs from different places then play at more nearly the same volume.

It sets the loudest peak, not how loud a song sounds overall, so songs may still sound different.

To Trim Silence at the Ends

Turn on “Trim silence at the ends,” and choose 2 or 3 seconds in Longer than.

Silence longer than that at the start or the end is cut down to half a second. Silence in the middle of a song is never touched.

To Change the Normalise Level

Click Advanced, near the foot of the window, to open it.

Click the arrows beside Normalise to.

The level goes from −6.0 to 0.0 dBFS, half a step at a time. The window’s description of Normalise changes to match.

Saving

The Converter saves everything in one folder, and never replaces a file without asking.

To Choose Where Files Are Saved

Under Save To, click Choose, select a folder, and click Open.

To go back to the MacMusic Converter folder in your Music folder, click Use Default. That folder is made the first time it’s needed.

If a File Is Already There

A mark beside a name in Saves as means the folder already has a file of that name. When you click Convert, the Converter asks:

  • Keep Both saves the new file under the next free name: Chill Dreamy 2.wav, say.
  • Replace puts the new file in place of the old one.
  • Cancel makes nothing.

The song you’re converting from is never replaced: if it has the same name, the new file takes the next free one.

While It Works

A conversion takes a few seconds, or a minute or two from YouTube. The foot of the window says what’s happening.

To Cancel a Conversion

Click Cancel, or press Esc.

It stops, and nothing half-made is kept.

If You Quit While It Works

The Converter asks first:

  • Keep Converting carries on.
  • Stop and Quit stops it and quits. The file being made isn’t saved; anything already finished is kept.

CHECK: a screenshot of this question is wanted.

Afterwards

Results, at the foot of the window, shows what was made.

To Find the Files

Click Show in Finder beside a file in Results.

A Finder window opens with the file selected.

Converter Reference

This section describes each part of the Converter’s window, what happens when you convert a song, and the menus.

The Converter Window

Local File and YouTube

Choose where the song comes from: a sound file on your Mac, or a YouTube video. The rest of the window follows your choice.

Source

With Local File: the song’s name and folder, what it is (its kind, samples a second, mono or stereo, and length), and Choose File or Choose Another. If the file can’t be read, or holds no sound, it says so here.

YouTube Video

With YouTube: the box for the address, Look Up, and the video’s title, channel and length once it’s found, or why it can’t be used.

Output

Make: MacMusic WAV, MP3 or Both. Title: the title MacMusic shows. File name: what the files are called (empty: named after the title). Saves as: the names they’ll have, with a mark if a file of that name is already there.

Sound

Normalise; Trim silence at the ends; Longer than (2 or 3 seconds).

Save To

The folder the files are saved in, with Choose and, if you’ve chosen one, Use Default.

Advanced

Click it to open or close it: see “Advanced” below.

Results

After a conversion: what was done to the sound (normalised by how much, trimmed by how much), and each file made, with its length, size and loudest peak, and Show in Finder. If something went wrong, the message is here.

Convert and Cancel

Convert starts (or press Return); it’s dimmed until there’s a song to convert. While it works, the foot of the window shows what it’s doing, a progress bar, and Cancel (or press Esc).

What Happens When You Convert

The foot of the window names each stage as it comes:

StageWhat it does
Retrieving the audio from YouTubeWith YouTube only: fetches the video’s sound, not its pictures.
Checking the fileReads the song, and makes sure it holds sound.
Analysing the soundFinds its loudest peak and any silence at the ends, if Normalise or Trim is on.
Making the MacMusic WAV, Making the MP3Makes each file, in a hidden place in the folder.
Checking the resultsChecks each file is what it should be (a MacMusic WAV by MacMusic’s own rules) before giving it its name.

Nothing is given its name until it’s been checked, so a file you see in the folder is always complete.

Advanced

Normalise to

The level Normalise brings the loudest peak to: −6.0 to 0.0 dBFS, in half steps; −1.0 to start with.

MacMusic WAV

Fixed: 22,050 Hz, mono, 8-bit, what MacMusic plays.

MP3

192 kbps, stereo if the song is. An MP3 made from a compressed song (an MP3, or YouTube’s sound) is no better than the song.

FFmpeg, yt-dlp and Deno

The programs inside the Converter that do the work: FFmpeg reads and makes the sound, yt-dlp and Deno fetch from YouTube. Their versions are shown here; see Appendix D.

Version

The Converter’s version, 1.0. Licences shows the licences of the programs inside it (CHECK: its window isn’t yet pictured).
