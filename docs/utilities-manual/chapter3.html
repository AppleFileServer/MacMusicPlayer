Introduction

The MacMusic Transfer Utility takes your MacMusic WAV files to the Macintosh Plus. It puts them on a disk the Plus can use: a disk image, which the BlueSCSI in the Plus reads from its SD card as though it were another hard disk.

You copy the disk image onto the card yourself, with the Finder; the Transfer Utility never writes to the card.

What it makes

A disk called Transfer, holding a folder called WAV Files with your songs in it, kept in one file on your Mac, such as HD60_512 Transfer.hda. Its name tells the BlueSCSI what it is: a hard disk at SCSI ID 6 (Appendix B, “Disk Images”). It’s made the size the songs need, with a quarter as much room again for more.

SCSI IDs

Each disk on the Plus has a SCSI ID, 0 to 6 (7 is the Macintosh itself), and each disk image on the card needs an ID of its own. Your startup disk is usually 0. The Transfer Utility suggests 6.

Safe with your files

It checks every song against MacMusic’s own rules first, and refuses any that aren’t MacMusic WAV files; it never changes a song; and it checks the finished disk, file by file, before it keeps it. Nothing half-made is left behind.

1Putting Songs on a Disk for the Plus

In this lesson you put songs made by the Converter on a disk image, copy it to the card, and find the songs on the Plus.

Open the MacMusic Transfer Utility.

Its window waits for songs.

Drag MacMusic WAV files onto the window, or a folder of them, or click Add Files.

Each song is listed with its name on the Mac, its format, length and size, and a tick if it can go on the disk.

Under Disk, the SCSI ID is 6, and the file name and size of the disk image are shown.

Click Build Transfer Image.

The Transfer Utility makes the disk, copies the songs onto it, and checks it, saying at the foot of the window what it’s doing.

When it’s done, Result shows the disk image, and what to do with it.

Click Show in Finder.

Shut the Plus down with Shut Down, switch it off, take the card out of the BlueSCSI, and put it in your Mac.

Drag the disk image onto the card, into its top level, not into a folder; then eject the card.

Put the card back in the BlueSCSI, and switch the Plus on.

A disk called Transfer appears on the Plus’s desktop, with your songs in its WAV Files folder.

Open the Transfer disk, and its WAV Files folder.

The songs show most clearly by name: choose by Name from the View menu.

Import them with MacMusic’s Import Song: see the MacMusic User’s Guide, Lesson 7.

2Adding More Songs Later

Later you’ll have more songs for the Plus. There are two ways.

  • On the Plus: the Transfer disk has room to spare (a quarter again), so songs can be copied onto it there, from a floppy disk, say.
  • A new disk image: add all the songs, old and new, to the Transfer Utility, and build again. It asks what to do with the image already in the folder: Replace it, or Keep Both.

To replace the disk on the card, drag the new image onto the card as before; the Finder asks whether to replace the old one, which has the same name.

MacMusic’s library on the Plus is separate from the Transfer disk: a song imported into it stays there, whatever becomes of the disk.

Choosing the Songs

Only MacMusic WAV files can go on the disk: those the Converter makes.

To Add Songs

Drag WAV files, or a folder of them, onto the window.

OR

Click Add Files, choose them, and click Open.

A folder adds the WAV files in it, not those in folders inside it. The same file added twice is listed once.

To Remove Songs

Select them in the list and click Remove; or click Clear All.

The files themselves aren’t touched.

If a Song Can’t Be Used

Its Status says Can’t be used; point to it to see why: it isn’t a WAV file, or isn’t in MacMusic’s format (mono, 22,050 Hz, 8-bit).

Remove it, or make a MacMusic WAV of it with the Converter, and add that instead.

The disk isn’t built while a refused song is in the list.

If a Name Is Changed

A Macintosh file name is at most 31 characters, without colons, and two names that differ only in capitals count as the same. Where a name has to change, the new one is shown in orange under On the Mac: a long name shortened, a colon made a hyphen, a second “Clash.wav” beside “clash.wav” made “Clash 2.wav,” “Rock: Live.wav” made “Rock- Live.wav.” The file on your Mac keeps its name.

The Disk

The disk’s SCSI ID, its name and its size are under Disk.

To Choose the SCSI ID

Choose an ID from the SCSI ID menu: 0 to 6.

The file name changes to match: HD50_512 Transfer.hda for 5. Choose an ID no other disk image on the card uses.

ID 0 is usually your startup disk’s: if you choose it, you’re asked to confirm (Use ID 0 Anyway), since putting the image on the card would replace your startup disk.

How the Size Is Worked Out

The songs’ size, plus a quarter as much again for adding more, plus 4 MiB for the disk’s own needs, rounded up to whole megabytes; at least 10 MiB, and at most 2 GB, the largest disk System 6 can use. Click “How is this worked out?” under Size to see the sums.

Sizes are shown as the Finder shows them (46.1 MB). On the Plus, sizes are in K of 1,024 bytes, so the same disk shows there as about 45,000K.

To Choose Where It’s Saved

Under Save To, click Choose and choose a folder. Use Default goes back to the MacMusic Transfer folder in your Music folder.

If an Image Is Already There

If the folder already has an image of that name, you’re asked:

  • Keep Both saves the new one as “HD60_512 Transfer 2.hda.” Both use the same SCSI ID, so put only one on the card.
  • Replace puts the new one in place of the old.
  • Cancel builds nothing.

Building

Building makes the disk image, copies the songs onto it, and checks it, in a minute or so.

To Build the Disk Image

Click Build Transfer Image.

The foot of the window says what it’s doing, with a progress bar and Cancel. When it’s done, Result shows the image, its size and the room left on it, and Verified.

To Cancel

Click Cancel, or press Esc.

Nothing is saved.

If you quit while it’s building, you’re asked first: Keep Building, or Stop and Quit. Stop and Quit leaves your songs, and any disk image already finished, as they are.

To See What Was Checked

Click “What was checked” under Result.

It lists each check the finished disk passed: its layout, its volume, and each song, byte for byte. Copy Summary copies the lot, to keep or to send. Any name that had to change is listed at the end.

On the Card and the Plus

The Transfer Utility’s work ends on your Mac; the last steps are yours.

To Copy the Disk Image to the Card

Shut the Plus down with Shut Down, and switch it off.

Take the card out of the BlueSCSI, and put it in your Mac.

Click Show in Finder, and drag the image onto the card’s top level.

Eject the card in the Finder, and put it back in the BlueSCSI.

Before you copy it, think of backing the card up with the Macintosh Backup Utility (Chapter 4).

To Use the Disk on the Plus

Switch the Plus on.

The Transfer disk appears on the desktop. Open it, and its WAV Files folder, to see the songs.

In MacMusic, choose Import Song from the File menu, and open the WAV Files folder (MacMusic User’s Guide, “Adding Songs”).

If the Disk Doesn’t Appear

See Appendix C, “The disk doesn’t appear.” Most often another image on the card has the same SCSI ID, or the image went into a folder on the card rather than its top level.

Transfer Utility Reference

This section describes each part of the Transfer Utility’s window, what happens when you build, and Advanced.

The Transfer Utility Window

Files

The songs: each file’s name, its name on the Mac (orange if it had to change), its format, length and size, and its status (a tick, or Can’t be used); Add Files, Remove, Clear All; how many, how long and how big altogether.

Disk

SCSI ID (0 to 6; 0 marked as your boot disk); the image’s file name; the reminder that each ID needs its own image; the volume (“Transfer”, its songs in “WAV Files”); the size, with How is this worked out?

Save To

The folder the image is saved in, Choose and Use Default.

Advanced

The disk template (below).

Result

After building: the image, the number of songs, its size, the room left on it, Verified; Copy Summary and Show in Finder; what to do next; What was checked.

Build Transfer Image

Starts building; dimmed until there are songs and none is refused. While it builds: what it’s doing, a progress bar, and Cancel.

What Happens When You Build

StageWhat it does
Checking the filesEach song checked again, as it is now.
Preparing the diskThe image made, its start copied from the disk template.
Formatting the HFS volumeThe Transfer disk made inside it, as a Macintosh disk.
Copying the filesEach song copied into WAV Files, under its Mac name.
Verifying the diskThe whole disk read back and checked, song by song, byte for byte.
FinishingOnly then is the image given its name.

Advanced

Disk template

The start of every disk the Transfer Utility makes: Apple’s partition map and hard disk driver, which the Plus needs to use a hard disk. The Transfer Utility has its own; Choose uses another BlueSCSI disk image’s, and Use Default goes back.
