Introduction

Everything on your Macintosh Plus lives on the BlueSCSI’s SD card: its disks, with the System, your programs, and MacMusic and its songs. Cards wear out, and a power cut while the Plus is using the card can damage a disk on it. The Macintosh Backup Utility keeps copies of the card on your Mac, so that whatever happens to the card, its disks can be put back as they were.

It works rather as Time Machine does for your Mac: each backup is kept, dated, and older ones are thinned out as time goes on.

Snapshots

Each backup is a snapshot: a complete copy of everything on the card at that moment, in a folder of its own on your Mac, with a record (its manifest) of every file and a check-sum of each. A snapshot stands on its own: you can restore from it even if every other snapshot were lost.

Safe with your card

The Backup Utility never writes to the card it backs up. It reads every file, copies it, and checks the copy against what it read; only a backup in which every file checks becomes a snapshot. It never deletes a backup unless you ask, or under the thinning rule you can turn off.

Where the backups go

In a folder called Mac Plus Backups, in your home folder, unless you choose another in Settings. A card of 2 GB of disks takes about 2 GB for each snapshot, so keep an eye on the room on your Mac.

1Backing Up Your Card

In this lesson you back up the card for the first time, and register it, so the Backup Utility knows it from then on.

Shut the Macintosh Plus down: choose Shut Down from the Special menu, and switch it off when it says you may.

The card must never come out while the Plus is using it. Shut Down puts every disk away properly first.

Take the card out of the BlueSCSI and put it in your Mac’s card slot (or a card reader).

Open the Macintosh Backup Utility.

It recognises a BlueSCSI card by its disks and its settings file, and asks to register it the first time. (CHECK: the registration sheet isn’t yet pictured.)

Type a name for the card, or keep “Mac Plus BlueSCSI”, and click Register.

The window lists the card’s disks and other files.

Click Back Up Now, the first button in the toolbar.

A sheet says what will be copied and how much room it needs, and what’s free.

Click Back Up.

The Backup Utility copies each file, checking each as it goes. A card of 2 GB takes a few minutes; the window says where it’s got to.

When it’s done, it says “Backed up and verified,” and the snapshot appears in the history on the right.

Click Eject, put the card back in the BlueSCSI, and switch the Plus on.

Put the card back before you switch the Plus on, never after.

2Restoring a Disk Image

Say one of the Plus’s disks has gone wrong. Here you get it back from a snapshot, as it was then.

In the history, select the snapshot to restore from.

Its contents are listed below it, its disks first.

Click Restore… in the toolbar.

Click “Chosen files and folders,” and tick the disk image.

Click Choose, choose a folder on your Mac, and click Restore.

The disk image is copied into the folder, and checked against the snapshot. The sheet says how many files were restored; click Done.

Shut the Plus down, put its card in your Mac, and drag the restored disk image onto the card, replacing the damaged one.

Only you ever change the card: the Backup Utility doesn’t write to a card that holds anything. If a card itself has failed, see “To Restore Onto a New Card” in the tasks that follow.

Eject the card, put it back in the BlueSCSI, and switch the Plus on.

Backing Up

A backup copies everything on the card into a new snapshot, and checks every file. It never starts by itself.

To Back Up the Card

Shut the Plus down with Shut Down, and switch it off.

Put the card in your Mac.

The window says the card is connected.

Click Back Up Now, then Back Up.

If the Backup Utility is in the background when the card goes in, a notification says the card is connected.

If a Disk Wasn’t Shut Down Cleanly

A disk records whether it was put away properly. If one on the card wasn’t (the power failed while the Plus was using it, say), the Backup Utility warns you before the backup, and marks the snapshot in the history.

The backup goes ahead: it keeps the disk as it is. An earlier snapshot, from before the power cut, may be the better one to restore from.

To Cancel a Backup

Click Cancel, or press Esc.

It stops. What it had copied isn’t kept as a snapshot; your earlier snapshots aren’t touched.

If the Backup Isn’t Verified

The window says why, and the job isn’t kept as a snapshot:

  • The card changed during the backup: a file was different afterwards. Make sure nothing else is using the card, and back up again.
  • The backup failed: a file couldn’t be read or written; the message names it.
  • The card was removed during the backup.

Each leaves an incomplete job, listed under the history; see “To Clear Incomplete Jobs.”

Your Card

The Backup Utility remembers each card you register, by name, and keeps its snapshots together.

To Register a Card

The first time a card is inserted, the Backup Utility asks to register it.

Type a name, such as Mac Plus BlueSCSI, and click Register.

OR

Click Not Now, and register it later with Register… in the window.

Nothing is written to the card: the names are kept in the backup folder.

If It Asks Whether It’s the Same Card

A card that’s been reformatted looks new to your Mac, though it has the same name, size and format. The Backup Utility asks.

Click “Yes, the Same Card” to keep its backups together, or “No, a Different Card” to register it as a new one.

To Eject the Card

Click Eject, at the top of the window, after the backup.

OR

Eject it in the Finder.

Always eject the card before you take it out of your Mac.

The History

The history lists the snapshots, newest first, and shows what the selected one holds.

To See What a Snapshot Holds

Select the snapshot.

Its files are listed below: the disk images first, then the settings and log, with macOS’s own hidden files last.

To see its folder, click Browse Backups in the toolbar.

To Check a Snapshot

Select it and click Verify Backup in the toolbar.

Every file is read again and checked against the snapshot’s record. It says “Verified: 13 files match,” or names each file that doesn’t, and how.

To Keep a Snapshot

Control-click the snapshot, and choose Keep (Never Thin).

It’s marked with a pin, and thinning never removes it. Choose Don’t Keep to take the mark off.

To Delete a Snapshot

Control-click it, choose Delete…, and click Move to Trash.

It goes to the Trash, so it can be put back until the Trash is emptied. If it’s the card’s only backup, you’re told so first.

To Clear Incomplete Jobs

Backups that failed, were cancelled, or were interrupted (if your Mac stopped during one) are listed under the history as incomplete jobs. They hold no snapshot.

Click Clear…, then Move to Trash.

Restoring

Restoring copies files out of a snapshot, and checks each against the snapshot’s record. It never writes to a card that holds anything.

To Restore Files to a Folder

Select a snapshot, and click Restore….

Click “Chosen files and folders,” and tick them.

Click Choose, choose a folder, and click Restore.

If a file of the same name is there, you’re asked: Keep Both, Replace or Skip, with “Do the same for the rest.” Skip leaves the file there as it is.

To Restore the Whole Card to a Folder

Click Restore…, keep “The whole snapshot, into a new folder,” choose where, and click Restore.

A new folder, “Restored” and the snapshot’s name, holds everything the card did.

To Restore Onto a New Card

If the card itself has failed, a snapshot can be put onto a new one.

Format the new card in Disk Utility: Erase, Format ExFAT, Scheme Master Boot Record. (The Backup Utility never formats a card.)

Insert it, Control-click the snapshot, and choose Restore to a New Card….

Choose the new card (“Empty, and ready”), click Restore to Card…, and confirm.

Every file is written and checked. Then click “Use as Mac Plus BlueSCSI’s Replacement,” so the new card carries on the old one’s name and backups, and Eject.

Older Snapshots and Settings

After each backup, older snapshots of the card are thinned out, so the backups don’t fill your Mac.

How Older Snapshots Are Thinned

For each card, the Backup Utility keeps:

  • the newest snapshot of each day, for a week;
  • the newest of each week, for a month;
  • the newest of each month, for a year.

It always keeps at least two, and never one marked Keep. Where it can, it keeps one whose disks were all shut down cleanly. Those removed go to the Trash, and the window says which.

To Change the Settings

Choose Settings from the Macintosh Backup Utility menu (or press Command-comma).

To keep backups elsewhere, click Choose… beside Backup folder; Use Default goes back to Mac Plus Backups.

To keep every snapshot, turn off “Thin older snapshots after each backup.”

Backup Utility Reference

This section describes the window, what happens when you back up, and where the backups are kept.

The Backup Utility Window

Status

What’s happening: waiting for a card (with the reminder to shut the Plus down first), the card connected, backing up (with the file it’s on, and Cancel), or the result. The last backup’s date is on the right, with Eject when the card can be ejected.

Toolbar

Back Up Now; Browse Backups (the snapshot’s folder in the Finder); Verify Backup; Restore…; Open Backup Folder; Settings.

The card

Its registered name, its own name, where it is, its format and how full it is; Choose Card (to choose a card or folder yourself) and Forget.

What’s on it

Every file the card holds, its disk images first, and any warnings: a disk not shut down cleanly, a file that can’t be read.

History

The snapshots: date, card, files, size, the room each takes on your Mac, and whether it’s verified (with a warning sign if a disk in it wasn’t shut down cleanly); a pin for one marked Keep. Below: how many, how much room, and any incomplete jobs. Control-click a snapshot for Restore…, Restore to a New Card…, Verify Backup, Show in Finder, Keep and Delete….

What the snapshot holds

The selected snapshot’s files, each with its kind, size and whether it was verified, and the result of Verify Backup.

What Happens When You Back Up

StageWhat it does
Looking at the cardLists everything on it, reading only; reads each disk’s own record of whether it was shut down cleanly.
CopyingCopies each file, a piece at a time, working out its check-sum (SHA-256) as it reads; then reads the copy back to check it.
Checking the card hasn’t changedLooks at every file on the card again: any that changed or went makes the backup unverified.
FinishingWrites the snapshot’s record, reads it back, and only then gives the snapshot its name; then thins older ones.

Where the Backups Are

In the backup folder (Mac Plus Backups, unless you chose another):

FolderHolds
Snapshotsa folder for each snapshot, named by its date and time (2026-10-09_18-11-03_3d70), holding Files, the card’s copy, and SnapshotManifest.json, its record
Catalogthe index of snapshots (rebuilt from the snapshots if it’s ever lost), the registered cards, and the snapshots marked Keep
Logsfor the Backup Utility’s own use

A snapshot’s Files folder is an ordinary folder: you can copy a disk image straight out of it in the Finder. Don’t change anything inside a snapshot: Verify Backup would find it no longer matches.

Limits

What’s backed upThe card’s files and folders, not the card itself (its partition table, free space or deleted files)
Each snapshotA full copy; only never-used space inside a disk image is left out
CardsexFAT or FAT32, as BlueSCSI uses
A new cardEmpty, exFAT (or FAT32, if no file is 4 GB or more), big enough
