MacMusic Utilities

About This Manual

MacMusic plays music on a Macintosh Plus. The MacMusic utilities are three programs for your Mac, a modern Mac, that look after that music: they make your songs into MacMusic’s sound files, take them to the Plus, and keep a safe copy of what’s there. This manual shows you how.

  • The MacMusic Converter Utility makes a song, from a sound file or a YouTube video, into a MacMusic WAV file, and an MP3 if you like.
  • The MacMusic Transfer Utility puts MacMusic WAV files on a disk for the Plus: a disk image that the Plus’s BlueSCSI reads from its SD card.
  • The Macintosh Backup Utility keeps copies of the BlueSCSI’s SD cards on your Mac, rather as Time Machine does for your Mac, so a disk can be put back if something on the Plus is lost. (CHECK: it’s described here as planned.)

What’s on the USB drive

The USB drive in the box holds:

  • the three utilities;
  • this manual and the MacMusic User’s Guide, to read on your Mac;
  • the MacMusic disk, for the Macintosh Plus;
  • the utilities’ source code and licences;
  • a Read Me.

CHECK: the drive’s contents are settled when it’s made (UB8).

What you need

  • a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26;
  • a Macintosh Plus with System 6.0.7 and MacMusic 1.0;
  • a BlueSCSI in the Plus, and a way to read its SD card on your Mac;
  • for YouTube, an internet connection.

Other MacMusic manuals

The MacMusic User’s Guide explains MacMusic itself: importing the songs this manual helps you make, and playing them. This manual names it wherever you’ll need it.

What you should know

This manual assumes you know your Mac: opening programs, using the Finder and its folders, and copying files. It also assumes you know the Macintosh Plus, as the MacMusic User’s Guide and the Macintosh Owner’s Guide teach it.

Two computers

The utilities run on one computer, and their work ends up on another. This manual always says which: your Mac is the modern one; the Macintosh Plus, or the Plus, is the one that plays MacMusic.

How to use this manual

  • Start here: Chapter 1, “Getting Started,” explains what’s on the USB drive, installs the utilities, and follows one song from your Mac to the Plus.
  • Each program: Chapters 2 to 4 take one utility each: “Converting Songs,” “Moving Songs to the Macintosh” and “Backing Up Your Macintosh.” Each has a lesson or two, the tasks it does one at a time, and a reference to every part of its window.
  • When something goes wrong: the appendixes list every message and what to do, and describe the files the utilities make.

The glossary explains the words this manual uses, and the index finds any subject.

Glossary

Apple silicon

The kind of processor in Macs made since 2020. The utilities need one.

BlueSCSI

A small board that plugs into the Macintosh Plus’s SCSI port and acts as one or more hard disks, reading disk images from an SD card.

dBFS

Decibels below full scale: how loud a sound is compared with the loudest a file can hold. 0 dBFS is the most; −1.0 dBFS is just below it.

disk image

A whole disk kept in one file. The Transfer Utility makes them; a BlueSCSI reads them.

disk template

The start of a BlueSCSI disk image (its partition map and Apple’s hard disk driver), which the Transfer Utility copies at the start of every disk it makes.

Finder

The program that shows your files, folders and disks, on your Mac and on the Macintosh Plus alike.

HFS

The Macintosh’s own way of keeping files on a disk, which System 6 uses. A Transfer Utility disk is an HFS disk.

import

In MacMusic, to add a song to your library, with Import Song from the File menu (MacMusic User’s Guide).

kbps

Thousands of bits a second: how much an MP3 uses for each second of sound. The Converter’s MP3s are 192 kbps.

MacMusic WAV

The one kind of sound file MacMusic plays: a WAV file, mono, with 22,050 samples a second, 8 bits each. The Converter makes them.

mono

Sound on one channel. MacMusic plays in mono, since the Macintosh has one sound channel; a stereo song is mixed into one.

MP3

A kind of sound file that takes much less room than a WAV file, for listening on your Mac or elsewhere. MacMusic doesn’t play them.

normalise

To make a song louder or quieter, so that its loudest moment reaches a set level.

peak

A song’s loudest moment.

re-encode

To make an MP3 from a song that’s already an MP3 (or another compressed kind), which loses a little quality.

Results

The part of the Converter’s window that shows what a conversion made.

sample rate

How many times a second a sound is measured: 22,050 for a MacMusic WAV, 44,100 for a CD.

Saves as

In the Converter, the names the files will have, shown before you convert.

SCSI ID

A number, 0 to 7, that tells apart the devices on the Macintosh Plus’s SCSI port. 7 is the Macintosh itself; a startup disk is usually 0. Each disk image on a BlueSCSI’s card has one.

SD card

The small memory card a BlueSCSI keeps its disk images on. You copy disk images to it on your Mac.

title

A song’s name as MacMusic shows it. The Converter puts it inside the WAV file.

trim silence

In the Converter, to cut long silence at the start or end of a song down to half a second.

WAV file

A common kind of sound file. MacMusic plays one kind of WAV file only: see MacMusic WAV.

YouTube address

A video’s web address, from your browser’s address bar, such as https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=….
